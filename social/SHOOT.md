# The one-visit shoot

One trip to Road No. 13, Plot 75A. About 90 minutes on the floor. What you come
back with has to carry three months of posting, so the list below is ordered to
walk the factory once, in the direction board actually travels, and never
double back.

You are not making a film. You are collecting **raw material** — 22 clips that
get cut up later. Nothing has to be beautiful. It has to be sharp, steady, close,
and long enough to cut into.

---

## Before you leave the house

| Do | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Wipe the lens with a cloth** | One fingerprint softens every clip. This is the single biggest quality difference and it costs five seconds. |
| **Free up 25 GB** | 4K fills a phone fast. Running out at shot 18 is the common failure. |
| **Charge to 100%, carry a power bank** | Video at 4K drains roughly 1% a minute. |
| **Settings → Camera → 4K, 30fps** | If 4K is not there, 1080p 60fps. 60fps lets us slow a shot to half speed later, which is how the die-cut and stitcher shots get their weight. |
| **Turn on grid lines** | Settings → Camera → Grid. Keeps horizons level without thinking. |
| **Airplane mode** | A call landing mid-shot kills the take and you will not notice until you are home. |

Wear closed shoes and short or rolled sleeves. Tell the operators before you
start that you are filming the machines, not them — people relax and work
normally once they know, and normal work is the whole point.

---

## Five rules that do all the work

1. **Everything vertical.** Hold the phone upright. Reels, Shorts, WhatsApp
   Status and LinkedIn all take vertical. One rule, no decisions on the floor.
   *(If time is left at the end, redo the five ★ shots horizontally for YouTube.)*

2. **Lock the exposure.** Tap the screen on the thing you care about, then
   **press and hold** until `AE/AF LOCK` appears. Factory light is uneven — an
   unlocked phone hunts and the clip pulses brighter and darker. Re-lock for
   every shot.

3. **Fifteen seconds, minimum, per shot.** Start recording, then count slowly to
   fifteen before you stop. It will feel absurdly long. Every editor's actual
   problem is clips that are too short to use. Long clips can be cut; short ones
   cannot be grown.

4. **Get closer than feels right, and do not zoom.** Digital zoom is just
   cropping and it destroys the image. Walk in. The best shots on this list are
   from about 40 cm away. Fill the frame with the board.

5. **Do not move the phone.** No panning, no following, no walking. Brace both
   elbows against your ribs and stand still — the machine supplies the movement.
   Three shots below are marked *slow push*; those are the only ones where you
   move, and you move about 20 cm over the full fifteen seconds.

---

## Two things not to film

- **Customer branding.** Printed boxes carry other companies' names and artwork.
  Posting those puts a client relationship on the internet without asking.
  Wherever there is a choice, film **plain kraft** or a job whose owner has said
  yes. If a printed name lands in an otherwise great shot, keep the shot and flag
  it — it can be blurred, but only if we know.
- **Faces, without asking first.** Hands are better anyway: hands on board read as
  skill, faces read as a staged photo. Ask before any shot where someone is
  recognisable, and if the answer is no, shoot their hands.

## The walk — 22 shots

Work down the list in order. Tick each one off. **★ = do a horizontal pass too if
time allows.**

### At the paper store

**1. The reels ★** — *15s, static, wide*
Stand back far enough to get three or four kraft reels in frame, stacked or on
end. Get the scale in: if a person walks past, better.
> Feeds: "What GSM actually tells you", and the opening frame of almost everything.

**2. Reel end grain** — *15s, static, very close*
Right up to the flat end of one reel, so the spiral of wound paper fills the
frame. Beautiful, abstract, and unmistakably real.
> Feeds: cover frames, transitions between sections.

**3. A hand pulling the paper tail** — *15s, static, close*
Someone lifts the loose end of a reel and lets it fall. Kraft has a specific
weight to it — the clip shows a material, not a picture of one.

---

### At the corrugator

This is the machine nobody outside the trade has seen, and it is the most
valuable footage in the building. Spend real time here.

**4. Flute forming ★** — *20s, static, as close as safe*
The point where flat paper enters the fluting rolls and comes out wavy. Stand
where you can see the wave appear. Do not put your hand or the phone anywhere
near the rollers — frame it from outside the guard.
> Feeds: "What actually makes a box strong", "3 ply, 5 ply, 7 ply". This is the
> hero shot of the whole channel.

**5. Single-face web running** — *15s, static, close*
The finished single-face board travelling — flat liner on one side, visible
flutes on the other. Frame it so the flutes run diagonally across the screen.

**6. Glue line** — *15s, static, close*
Wherever adhesive meets paper. Even a modest glue unit looks industrial and
purposeful in close-up.

**7. Steam / heat off the hot plate** — *15s, static*
Only if visible. Heat shimmer or steam reads as *process happening* better than
any caption.

**8. The web disappearing into the machine** — *15s, slow push*
Start about a metre back, move in 20 cm across fifteen seconds. One of only three
moving shots.

---

### At the cutter and stacker

**9. Sheets dropping onto the stack ★** — *20s, static, mid*
The rhythm of sheet after sheet landing. Long clip — the repetition is the point,
and it cuts to music perfectly.
> Feeds: "Flat blank to finished box in eight seconds", every music-led edit.

**10. The stack edge, close** — *15s, static, very close*
The side of a finished stack of board, so you can count the flute layers along
the cut edge. 3 ply and 5 ply side by side if both are running.
> Feeds: "3 ply, 5 ply, 7 ply — which one you actually need". Essential.

**11. A hand squaring the stack** — *15s, static, close*
Hands tapping a stack straight. Small human gesture, high credibility.

---

### At the printer

**12. Ink hitting board ★** — *20s, static, close*
The print roller meeting kraft, ideally where you see clean board go in and
printed board come out. Lock exposure on the printed area, not the light.
> Feeds: "Ink hitting board".

**13. The ink tray** — *15s, static, close*
Ink in the duct or tray. Colour against brown board is the strongest image in the
building.

**14. A printed sheet lifted into the light** — *15s, static*
Someone picks a sheet off the stack and holds it up to check registration.
Plain or approved artwork only.

---

### At creasing, slotting, die-cutting

**15. The creasing wheels** — *15s, static, very close*
The wheels pressing a fold line into board. Watch the board deform.

**16. Slotter knives cutting flaps** — *20s, static, close*
Flaps being cut. Repetitive, mechanical, satisfying.

**17. The die coming down ★** — *20s, static, close*
The platen closing on board. Shoot at 60fps if your phone offers it — at half
speed this is the most watchable four seconds on the channel.
> Feeds: "The die coming down", "The mailer box".

**18. Waste being stripped out** — *15s, static, close*
The offcut skeleton lifted away from the cut blanks. Nobody outside the trade
knows this step exists, which is exactly why it works.
> Feeds: "Nine stages" content and the waste/recycling angle.

---

### At stitching or pasting

**19. The stitcher firing ★** — *20s, static, close*
Wire stitches going into a corner, one after another. Sound matters here — see
below.
> Feeds: "Four reasons your cartons arrive crushed" (joint strength).

**20. A flat blank becoming a box** — *20s, static, mid*
One person taking a flat blank and folding it up into a formed box, start to
finish, in one unbroken take. Ask them to do it at normal speed, not slowly.
> Feeds: "Flat blank to finished box in eight seconds" — the single best
> stop-scrolling clip you can get. Shoot it **three times** from three distances.

---

### At the finished-goods area

**21. Bundles strapped and stacked ★** — *15s, static, wide*
A wall of finished bundles. Scale is the message: this is a plant, not a garage.

**22. Loading out** — *20s, slow push, mid*
Bundles going into a vehicle. Move in slowly across the take.
> Feeds: "Loading out", the delivery-area content, the closing frame of most
> videos.

---

## Sound — do not skip this

Thirty seconds, twice:

- **Stand still next to the corrugator, record 30 seconds of video pointed at
  the floor.** You are recording the *room*, not the picture.
- **Do the same next to the stitcher.**

Real machine noise underneath an edit is the thing AI cannot fake and stock
libraries do not have. It is thirty seconds of work and it changes how every
video lands.

## Stills, at the end

Switch to photo mode and take these. Two seconds each, and they become carousel
slides and cover frames for months.

1. Flute edge, extreme close-up, 3 ply — *fills the frame*
2. Flute edge, extreme close-up, 5 ply — *same distance, for a true comparison*
3. A single finished box, plain kraft, on a clean floor, shot straight on
4. The same box from a 45° corner
5. A stack of board from directly above
6. The bursting-strength tester dial, if the unit has one
7. Kraft reels, wide
8. The board edge showing the liner/flute/liner sandwich, lit from the side
9. Hands holding a flat blank
10. The loaded vehicle, wide

---

## If someone will talk to camera — three questions, 30 seconds each

Only if it is comfortable. Vertical, phone at chest height, person standing
still, machine running behind them. **Ask them to answer in one unbroken take
and to say the question back as part of the answer.** Telugu, Hindi or English —
subtitles get added later, and a real answer in Telugu outperforms a stiff one in
English every time.

1. "How do you know a box will hold before it leaves here?"
2. "What do customers get wrong most often when they order?"
3. "What is the hardest job you have run this month?"

## The moment you get home

1. **Copy everything to `C:\qualityenterprises\social\footage\raw\` before you do
   anything else.** Phones lose files.
2. Rename nothing. The pipeline reads whatever is there.
3. Run `node scripts/social/assets.mjs --index` to build the manifest, then flag
   any clip that caught customer branding.

Three months of content comes out of this folder. It is worth the ninety minutes.
